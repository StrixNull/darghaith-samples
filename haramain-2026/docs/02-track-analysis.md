## 2. لماذا فاز A1-1 رغم أنه الثالث في الترتيب المركّب

الترتيب المركّب يقيس الملاءمة العامة، أما الاختيار فتم بتحكيم المفاهيم التسعة (ثلاث زوايا لكل تحدٍّ من الثلاثة: زاوية الضيف، زاوية عمليات الهيئة، زاوية التقنية) وفق معايير التحكيم المعتادة (مشكلة حقيقية 25%، ملاءمة الحل 25%، الابتكار 20%، الحل التقني 15%، الاستدامة 10%، تجربة المستخدم 5%) ومن ثلاث عدسات: مسؤول في الهيئة، محكّم تقني، وممثل تجربة ضيوف الرحمن.

- **مراكز العناية (B2-3):** مفاهيمها الثلاثة (ركن الأُنس، استباق، قِرى) قوية وسهلة التجريب في مركز واحد، لكنها تتقاطع مع أدوات تملكها الهيئة فعلاً (40 شاشة ذكية متعددة اللغات، بث ترجمة بـ 17 لغة، رافد الحرمين، راصد، لوحات NFC، الخط 1966، الخرائط التفاعلية بالعنونة المترية)، فتُقرأ كتحسين تدريجي لا كفكرة تفتح ملفاً جديداً. نحتفظ بها مساراً ثانياً وامتداداً طبيعياً للمحرك نفسه.
- **سفر الإفطار (B2-1):** أثر كبير ومقاييس واضحة، لكن رمضان 1448 يبدأ نحو 8 فبراير 2027 بعد انتهاء مرحلة النموذج الأولي على الأرجح، والعمليات الأثقل في مكة حيث لا نستطيع الوجود يومياً، وأحد المفاهيم يتطلب تغييراً في التغليف لدى المزوّدين.
- **الوصول والانتظار للروضة (A1-1):** يجيب حرفياً على حاجة صرّح بها الملف (رسائل توجيهية استباقية بلغة الضيف قبل ساعات من موعده ترسم مساره من فندقه حتى موضع سجوده)، ويعالج السبب الذي يحدده الملف لنقاط الضغط (تفاوت سرعة التحقق)، ولا يلمس نسك، ويعمل دون اتصال، وميزة المدينة فيه قصوى (رصد ميداني يومي)، ومفاهيمه الثلاثة تكاملت في مفهوم واحد: قصة الضيف من «هونًا»، والمحرك والختم الموقّع والتوأم الرقمي من «أناة»، و«تحقق واحد وتحرك بالفوج» من «ساعة الفوج».

## 4. ملخص كل تحدٍّ: ما هو قائم، والفجوة، والاتجاهات

### B2-3 — مراكز العناية بالضيوف كقناة نوعية استباقية لتقديم الخدمات

**المشكلة في سطر:** Guest care centres (2 main + 19 sub-centres in Hajj season per Ajel/Almowaten May 2026) are reactive desks for inquiries, paper booklets and lost-and-found; the Authority wants them re-engineered into proactive, multilingual, self-service-plus-human stations that also feed live field signals to decision rooms.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** Reading the priority axes, the Authority wants two things it cannot say bluntly: (1) a cheap way to absorb the 'my phone is dead / no internet / Nusuk will not book' anxiety cases before they become crowd or permit incidents, and (2) a sensing layer: the centres already have staff talking to thousands of guests per day, but nothing structured flows back to the batching/operations rooms. The winning framing is not 'a nicer help desk' but 'a distributed field-intelligence and pre-emption network that happens to look like hospitality'. They also care about the noise problem (staff cannot hear guests) and about cultures that will not ask for help verbally, hence self-service channels inside the centre.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Authority guest care centres + 1966 hotline:** 24/7 centres offering spatial and ritual guidance, multilingual inquiry answering and lost-and-found; lost-and-found centre at the eastern plaza near Makkah Library; hotline 1966. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Entirely reactive; no structured capture of what guests ask, no self-service tier, no link to operations; the brief itself calls this the strategic gap. — SPA N2529814, PEP news 891, Ajel/Almowaten May 2026
- **Smart interactive maps system (Feb 2026):** 950+ points of interest in 13 service categories, 650+ QR codes, 100,000+ dynamic routes optimised by operational variables; interactive screens in 5 languages; mobile teams carry real-time translation devices; pilot in Ramadan 2026 with metric addressing and colour zones. *لماذا لا يكفي:* It is wayfinding infrastructure, not a service channel; it does not handle a guest whose phone is dead, does not triage problems, and does not collect complaints. A team solution should build on its QR/metric addressing rather than compete with it. — Sabq / Youm7 / Urdupoint, 25-26 Feb 2026
- **Nusuk app and Nusuk Care Centres:** 160+ digital services, permits, smart maps, 24/7 support via Nusuk Care Centres and 1966. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Assumes a working phone and connectivity; the brief's pain points are precisely the guests for whom Nusuk has failed (dead battery, no internet, cannot book). Family-under-one-account and gender mis-booking issues surface at the centres. — SPA N2116368, Saudi Times, Arab News
- **Unified e-services login (eservices.alharamain.gov.sa):** Single sign-on portal for the Authority's electronic services. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Back-office and citizen-facing web; nothing that turns a physical centre into a proactive channel or a sensing point. — Authority website

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** No public evidence (search) of a structured intake tool at the centres, of an offline-capable self-service tier, or of a feedback pipeline into the decision rooms. The interactive-maps launch gives a ready-made metric addressing and QR grid the team can piggyback on for 'where am I / where is the centre / print me a route slip'. A scope-guarded Claude agent that triages in the guest's language, with a deterministic Arabic rule fallback when offline, is exactly what this team has already shipped (Rafiq pattern). Sanctity is easy: centres are in courtyards and service sites, not prayer halls, so screens and speech are acceptable there.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **ركن الطمأنينة** (Reassurance Kiosk): A tablet/kiosk plus staff console inside each centre. The guest picks a language (or scans their Nusuk permit QR), describes the problem by voice or pictograms; a scope-guarded agent triages into fixed flows: permit help, lost item, dead-battery rescue (power bank loan tracked by permit code), medical hand-off, and a printed route slip using the Authority's metric address of the target gate or Rawdah holding point. Every case is logged anonymously by category, language and centre. *يفوز لأن:* Hits three named pain points at once (unaware of services, phone dead, cannot explain in language); demoable offline on a real kiosk; directly reuses what the team has built; the 'staff cannot hear' spatial factor is solved by text and pictograms rather than speech alone. *قد يخسر لأن:* Jury may see it as 'another chatbot on a tablet'; without an Authority integration for permit lookup the permit flows are simulated; language quality for Urdu/Bengali/Indonesian speech needs testing with real visitors.
- **نبض الضيف** (Guest Pulse): Turn the centres into a sensing network: staff record each interaction with one tap (issue type, gate, language, severity) and guests can leave a 10-second QR micro-survey; the stream feeds a live operations dashboard with per-centre/per-hour heatmaps and threshold alerts (for example 'gate 25 confusion reports x3 in 15 min') pushed to the batching room and to field supervisors. *يفوز لأن:* Speaks the Authority's own language in the priority axes ('field sensing points feeding decision rooms around the clock'); low hardware; the audit-log and security angle differentiates from generic survey tools; cheap to pilot in one centre at the Prophet's Mosque. *قد يخسر لأن:* Value depends on staff discipline in tagging; without real Authority data the dashboard runs on synthetic streams, which honest disclosure will reveal; less emotionally compelling for a jury than a guest-facing demo.
- **بطاقة الجيب** (Pocket Card Handoff): A proactive pre-arrival channel: when a permit is booked (or at the hotel/centre), the guest receives a printable wallet card and a lightweight offline-first PWA in their mother tongue holding permit time, gathering point, route, and a 'what to do if your phone dies' code. Any centre can scan or type the card code to see the guest's plan and help without the guest's phone. Centres also push awareness messages (timing, route changes) to the PWA hours before the slot. *يفوز لأن:* Targets the single most anxious scenario in the brief (missing rituals because the phone failed) and ties into A1-1's ask for messages 'hours before the permit time in their mother tongue'; makes the centre the start of the journey rather than the last resort. *قد يخسر لأن:* Requires a hook into permit issuance (Nusuk) to be real; a paper card is low-tech and could be read as trivial; privacy design must be airtight or the Authority will not touch it.

**المخاطر:** Permit and lost-and-found integrations will be simulated unless the Authority opens an API during the camp; the pitch must disclose this honestly. · Speech recognition in noisy courtyards for South-Asian and Southeast-Asian languages may underperform; pictogram and text paths must carry the demo. · A generic 'chatbot kiosk' impression if the sensing-network and offline-rescue features are not front and centre. · Centre staff workflow changes need Authority buy-in; a tool that adds tapping load without reducing work will be rejected.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** Minute 1: a 'guest' with a dead phone walks up to a real kiosk, picks Urdu, taps the pictogram for 'lost permit'; the agent looks up a demo permit by card code, prints a route slip with the metric address of the gathering point and lends a power bank logged to the code. Minute 2: switch to the operations dashboard: the case appears as a dot on the centre map; three more simulated 'gate confusion' reports arrive and a threshold alert fires to the supervisor's phone in Arabic. Minute 3: unplug the network cable, repeat a triage in Arabic to show the deterministic offline fallback, then show the audit log hash chain and the 'what is real vs simulated' slide.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** The team can visit the guest care centres and information points around the Prophet's Mosque courtyards daily, count and categorise inquiries by observation (with permission), photograph the current paper booklets and signage, time how long a guest stands at a desk, test the noise level with a phone dB meter, and interview non-Arabic-speaking visitors about what they would have asked. They can also verify the Feb 2026 QR/metric addressing rollout on site and design the route slip to match it.

### B2-1 — سفر الإفطار: رفع الكفاءة وتقليل الهدر

**المشكلة في سطر:** Ramadan iftar in both mosques (about 300,000 meals/day on average in the Grand Mosque per the brief) mismatches quantities with actual consumption, produces waste and leftovers on carpets, and forces laying, lifting and cleaning into a 15-20 minute window before the iqamah.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** The Authority already governs who may lay a sufra (permit portal via Ihsan and Nusuk Humanitarian) and already has surplus collection partners; what it lacks is a closed-loop operational brain: forecast demand per zone, ration what is laid, know in near-real time what was consumed and what remains, and choreograph the 3,000+ workers and 1,000 volunteers through the critical window. 'Governed digitally and in the field' and 'under one regulatory umbrella' signal that they want a platform they own, tying permits, providers, zones, volunteers and waste data together, while keeping dignity (no embarrassing distribution) and carpet cleanliness.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Authority iftar permit portal with Ihsan and Nusuk Humanitarian (Ramadan 1447):** Individuals and NGOs register, pay through Ihsan, and receive a permit; individuals limited to one sufra of 50 meals/day; NGOs minimum 10 sufras men and 10 women; supervised meal standards. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Governs supply entry, not consumption: no zone-level demand forecast, no rationing per guest, no field feedback on leftovers, and no coordination tool for the lift-and-clean window. — alharamain.gov.sa portal announcement; Ajel/Al-Mowaten/Makkah Newspaper Jan 2026; SPA N2521975 on Ihsan
- **Ekram Association and Grace (Hifz Al-Ni'mah) surplus collection:** Volunteers and vehicles collect untouched surplus meals after iftar and redistribute them the next day; SPA reported 63,538 surplus items in the first 10 days of one Ramadan at the Grand Mosque. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Downstream rescue after waste has already been produced; does not reduce over-provisioning or the carpet-cleaning burden, and depends on manual counting. — SPA N2072494 (Ekram at Grand Mosque); SPA N2521482 (nearly 23,000 surplus meals collected at the Prophet's Mosque); Arab News 'Ramadan food leftovers going to the needy'
- **Authority field operation: 3,200 daily sufras, cleanup targeted within minutes:** Large trained workforce laying disposable sufras and clearing them before the iqamah. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Heroic manual effort; quantities are set by provider permits rather than measured demand, and the brief reports guests taking extra out of fear of running out. — Saudi Gazette 'Over 200,000 iftar meals being distributed daily'; brief statistics

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** The whole chain from permit to consumed meal is un-instrumented. A permit-linked digital sufra ledger (what was laid where, what came back), zone demand forecasting from historical footfall, a volunteer choreography tool for the 15-20 minute window, and a rationed distribution model (guest-facing 'one pack per person, take what you need' with dignity) are all software the team can build and simulate. The Authority's own numbers (300,000 meals/day, 10.8M meals/season) give the impact story for free.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **ميزان السُّفرة: توقّع الطلب والحصص لكل منطقة** (Sufra Balance: per-zone demand forecasting and allotment): A planning engine that forecasts meals needed per zone per day from prior-year consumption, day-of-Ramadan curve, weather, and permitted sufras, then issues allotments to providers and a laying plan. After iftar, supervisors log leftovers per sufra with a two-tap form or photo count; the model recalibrates for the next day. Dashboard shows waste per zone trending to zero. *يفوز لأن:* Directly attacks the 'mismatch between quantity and consumption' gap with a measurable KPI (surplus percentage) and slots into the existing permit portal; explainable models suit a governance-minded client. *قد يخسر لأن:* Without real Authority consumption data the forecast is synthetic; a jury may ask for validated accuracy the team cannot show before Ramadan 1448 (expected around February 2027).
- **نافذة العشرين دقيقة: تنسيق الرفع والتنظيف** (The Twenty-Minute Window: lift-and-clean choreography): A field coordination app for the 3,000+ workers and volunteers: each sufra row has a QR; teams check in, receive timed tasks (lay, distribute, lift, vacuum, inspect) synchronised to the adhan countdown, and report row status. Supervisors see a live carpet-clearance map and can redirect crews to lagging rows; the log yields honest time-to-clear metrics. Works on cheap Android phones and with no network via local queueing. *يفوز لأن:* Engineers the 'very critical 15-20 minute window' the brief names as the core challenge; demoable as a live simulation with the audience playing crews. *قد يخسر لأن:* Field crews are largely low-literacy contract workers; app adoption in a frantic window is a real adoption risk, and a jury from operations will know it.
- **وجبة بحصّة: توزيع مقنَّن كريم ومتابعة الفائض لحظياً** (Rationed Meal: dignified per-guest allotment and live surplus routing): Standardised meal packs with a simple tear-off token (or Nusuk permit scan for permitted visitors) so each guest receives one right-sized pack, plus a 'keep it' pouch so untouched extras are not left on the carpet. Uneaten sealed packs are scanned at collection points and routed live to Ekram-type partners, closing the loop between the Authority, providers and charities in one dashboard. *يفوز لأن:* Addresses the human factor (fear of running out, embarrassment) and the charities' need for counts; 'zero unsightly leftovers' becomes a packaging plus data story. *قد يخسر لأن:* Packaging and token logistics are physical changes the Authority may resist; scanning packs in a crowd is slow and can look bureaucratic.

**المخاطر:** Ramadan 1448 likely begins in early February 2027 (assumption based on calendar arithmetic); the prototype stage may close before the team can test in a real iftar. · Strong incumbents: Ihsan, Nusuk Humanitarian and Ekram already occupy the governance and surplus space; the entry must clearly extend rather than duplicate them. · Real consumption data is held by the Authority; forecasts will be synthetic until they share it. · Field adoption by contract workers and volunteers under time pressure is the hardest part and cannot be fully proven in a demo.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** A wall dashboard shows the Prophet's Mosque courtyard zones for 'Ramadan day 17'; the forecaster proposes allotments and shows last night's leftover percentage per zone. A countdown to maghrib starts; three judges with phones act as crew leads, scan row QRs, and the clearance map turns from red to green as they tap tasks, with the audit log streaming. After 'adhan', a supervisor logs 40 sealed surplus packs, which appear instantly on the charity partner's pickup screen. All offline-capable, running on a laptop.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** Good but time-bound: outside Ramadan the team can interview sufra organisers, Madinah charities and cleaning staff, study how permits work on the portal, and measure courtyard zone sizes; if the programme extends into February 2027 the team can observe actual iftar laying and clearing at the Prophet's Mosque and count leftovers by hand.

### A1-1 — الوصول والانتظار والدخول للروضة الشريفة

**المشكلة في سطر:** Around 50,000 permit holders a day (brief) spend an average of 20 minutes, and often more, in the courtyards and sorting points of the Prophet's Mosque not knowing which gate or gathering point to go to, how long remains, or what staff are shouting in Arabic; that uncertainty plus repeated permit checks turns the wait into pushing and pressure points at merging corridors instead of calm spiritual preparation.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** Reading the axes and factors together, the Authority is not asking for another booking app (Nusuk belongs to the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah, not the Authority, so it cannot rebuild it). It owns the field layer: the sorting points, the batching (تفويج) plan, the field staff, the barriers and the displays. It wants an operating model for the last 60 to 90 minutes before entry that it can pilot itself: (1) reassurance that kills the urge to push, i.e. every guest knowing in their mother tongue, hours before and again at the holding point, where to go, when, and that their slot is protected; (2) fewer and more uniform verification moments, because the brief says outright that any variance in verification speed creates a pressure point instantly, so pre-verification upstream or one-touch checks are the real safety lever; (3) a dignified, quiet way to fill the wait with preparation content so the final minutes at the Prophet's chamber are calm; (4) real-time integration with security and field agencies rather than overlapping authority; (5) measurable outcomes aligned to the Pilgrim Experience Program (wait variance, incidents at sorting points, satisfaction), with attention to women and the elderly. The organizational factor practically dictates one deliverable: proactive multilingual journey messages to the guest's phone hours before permit time mapping hotel to prayer spot. Note the field is الثقافة والقيم, so the jury will weigh guest experience and dignity above ops tooling; a pure dashboard risks being judged off-track.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Nusuk app Rawdah permit + المسار الفوري (immediate path):** Free instant permit with QR, men/women categories, one permit per visitor per 365 days; المسار الفوري launched early 2025 lets a visitor already in Madinah grab an empty slot, cancellable 6 hours before, one active permit at a time. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Booking-side only. Nothing between issuance and entry: no gate or gathering-point directions from the hotel, no live remaining-time, no message in the guest's language, no reassurance that the slot is protected. Guest voice in the brief shows gender mis-booking, waiting lists, family accounts and confusion about where the immediate route is. Third-party guide sites (e.g. nusukguide.com) exist precisely because the official flow stops at the QR. — https://haj.gov.sa/en/E-Services/Permit-to-Visit-Al-Rawdah-Al-Sharifiyah-for-Men-Women ; https://rahhal.wego.com/blog/زيارة-الروضة-الشريفة-تصريح-نسك-2026/
- **Authority project: تطوير وتحسين منظومة التفويج لزيارة الروضة الشريفة (6 stages):** The Authority's own batching system: sort paths, read QR, enter mosque and wait, move to Rawdah, pray, exit. Reported 3rd place in a customer-experience strategy award (search summary). *لماذا لا يكفي:* This is the baseline the brief itself criticises: heavy reliance on physical barriers and repeated permit checks, silent waiting, staff instructions in Arabic. It is an ops process, not a guest-facing experience. Any winning entry must plug into these 6 stages rather than replace them. — https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1151178
- **Authority initiative: الفرق الراجلة (walking guidance teams), March 2025:** Field teams on foot giving precise guidance with special attention to the elderly and people with disabilities. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Human-scale and language-limited; cannot reach 50,000 guests a day nor speak Urdu, Bengali, Indonesian, Turkish or Hausa at every holding point. A tool that multiplies these teams (one tap, many languages) is complementary, not competing. — Search summary citing alharamain.gov.sa / my.gov.sa (details unverified)
- **Springer 2025: Designing visitor journey for optimized visitor throughput, case study of Rawdah al Shareefah:** Simulation of partitioning the Rawdah (paper states roughly 750 visitors at a time) into smaller blocks; reports a 15% reduction in service time in simulation. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Simulation only, no deployed product, no guest-side experience, no waiting-point or language dimension. Useful as citable evidence that block-based batching works, and as a benchmark a student simulation can extend with real field timings. — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40558-025-00324-4
- **Emerald IJCS 2018 crowd dynamics of Al-Masjid Al-Nabawi + MassMotion simulation of the Ziara corridor:** Statistical characterisation of the visiting corridor (8 to 10 m wide, about 92 m long) at saturation, and a MassMotion study of queue optimisation and wait reduction in the Ziara corridor. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Academic and pre-Nusuk; describes the physics of the corridor but offers no operating model for successive waiting periods and nothing the guest experiences. — https://www.emerald.com/ijcs/article/2/1/52/114586 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311568768

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** The whole segment between permit issuance and stepping into the Rawdah is a black box for the guest, and that black box is where the fear, the pushing and the pressure points come from. Concretely: (1) there is no official mother-tongue pre-arrival journey (hotel to gate to gathering point) triggered by the permit time, although the brief names this as an organizational need; (2) at the holding point nobody tells the guest their batch position or remaining time, so a 20-minute wait feels open-ended; (3) staff instructions about queue organisation are spoken in Arabic and not understood by most foreign guests, which is a pain point the brief lists explicitly; (4) waiting is silent and empty, while the challenge text literally asks to convert it into cognitive and emotional preparation; (5) repeated permit checks add verification-speed variance, which the brief identifies as the instant cause of dangerous pressure; (6) women's flows are less studied and more stressful. The opportunity for this team is a field-layer product the Authority can pilot without touching Nusuk: a permit-time-driven, offline-capable, multilingual companion for guests plus a one-tap silent instruction broadcaster for field staff, backed by a discrete-event simulation calibrated with real timings that only a Madinah-based team can collect. The differentiator against the inevitable flood of app ideas is evidence and operating-model detail: real photos of the holding points, measured wait distributions, a canonical instruction set harvested from staff, and a demo that never depends on network.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **رفيق الروضة: رحلة مسبقة بلغة الضيف** (Rawdah Companion: mother-tongue pre-arrival journey and live holding-point status): A permit-time-driven journey channel that the Authority sends, not the guest searches for. At T-6h, T-2h and T-45min the guest receives, in the language of the permit (ar, en, ur, id, tr, bn, ms, fr, fa, ha as a first set), a mapped route from their hotel area to the correct gate and gathering point with real photos, what to bring, the rule that the slot is protected so there is no need to run, and a women-specific variant. On site, an offline-first single-file PWA shows batch position and an honest remaining-time estimate fed by the batching state, and at T-10min it switches to a calm preparation card. Delivery via WhatsApp Business API or Telegram for the demo with SMS fallback, since many foreign guests have no data in the courtyards. *يفوز لأن:* It is the deliverable the brief almost spells out (proactive messages in the mother tongue hours before, mapping hotel to prayer spot). Sanctity risk is near zero because everything is silent and on the guest's own phone. It fits the team's shipped skills exactly (FastAPI, RTL no-build UIs, deterministic engines, Telegram bots) and the Madinah location lets the team photograph and time every route and holding point for real content. Demoable end to end in 3 minutes with zero network. *قد يخسر لأن:* Without a Nusuk or Authority permit feed the permit-time trigger is simulated, and the jury may see it as a feature request for Nusuk rather than a pilotable product. Many competing teams will pitch some kind of app; if the team cannot show measured wait data and real holding-point content it will look generic. Phone battery and connectivity failures (brief B2-3) undercut any phone-only channel unless the SMS and printed-card fallbacks are real.
- **سكينة: الإرشاد الصامت المتزامن مع التفويج** (Sakinah: silent staff-to-guest instruction broadcast synchronised with the batch clock): Attacks two named pain points at once: guests not understanding staff instructions, and silent, anxious waiting. A field-staff tablet PWA offers about 20 canonical instructions harvested from real staff phrases on site (for example: batch 12, move to the right corridor; 10 minutes remaining, please sit; women's batch 3, prepare to move; your batch is protected, no need to push). One tap publishes the instruction to every guest phone registered at that holding point and to a kiosk-mode display page, each in the guest's own language, with no loudspeakers. A deterministic batch state machine (waiting, prepare, move, inside, exit) drives an Authority-approved preparation feed: etiquette of greeting, short supplications, where to stand, what will happen next, timed to the minutes left. Content is fixed and reviewed, never generated live. *يفوز لأن:* It is the most literal answer to the core challenge sentence (replace silent waiting with paths of cognitive and emotional enrichment) and to the safety axis, since uniform, understood instructions reduce the surges that happen when staff shout in one language. It respects sanctity by design: nothing is loud, nothing is animated, phones stay in hands. It multiplies the Authority's existing walking teams rather than replacing them, which makes it easy for the client to pilot. Very strong Madinah advantage: the instruction set and the timing come from field observation. *قد يخسر لأن:* Religious and etiquette content needs Sharia and Authority sign-off; if the team drafts it alone the jury may question governance. It requires guests to have opened the channel before reaching the holding point, so adoption depends on Direction 1 or on a printed QR at the gate. Staff behaviour change is an organisational sell, and the jury may ask why the Authority could not just add this to its own system.
- **نبض نقاط الفرز: استشعار الضغط ودعم قرار التفويج** (Sorting-Point Pulse: privacy-preserving occupancy sensing and batch-release decision support): An ops-room view that turns the brief's warning about verification-speed variance into a live signal. Inputs are either QR scan-rate timestamps per lane (no personal data) or anonymised Wi-Fi/BLE presence counts from small sensors at courtyard sorting points with hashed, non-stored identifiers. A simple queueing model predicts when each holding point saturates, flags a lane whose scan throughput drops (the pressure-point precursor), and recommends release timing or lane reassignment. Alerts go to field supervisors; a guest-facing side effect is a more honest remaining-time estimate for Directions 1 and 2. *يفوز لأن:* It is the only direction that speaks directly to the crowd-safety priority axis and to the organizational need for real-time integration between the Authority and security agencies. It uses the team's differentiating skills (IoT security, networking) that most student teams lack, and the courtyards are public enough to test counting outside the mosque. *قد يخسر لأن:* Hardware sensing inside or around the Prophet's Mosque raises privacy, security-clearance and procurement questions that a student team cannot settle, and the Diwan rule against bought external resources limits hardware. The challenge sits in the Culture and Values field, so an ops dashboard may be judged off-track. Without Authority scan data the model is calibrated only on what the team can observe from outside, and the demo is charts rather than a guest experience.
- **التوأم الرقمي للتفويج: نوافذ وصول متدرجة مثبتة بالمحاكاة** (Batching Digital Twin: simulation-backed staggered arrival windows and pre-verification policy): A discrete-event model of the whole pipeline (courtyard arrival, sorting, QR check, holding, transfer corridor, Rawdah, exit) calibrated with timings the team measures on site across prayer times and gender flows. It tests policies the Authority can actually choose: staggered arrival windows so a 50,000-permit day does not arrive in bursts at slot start, single pre-verification at a courtyard check-in instead of repeated checks, block partitioning as in the Springer 2025 paper, and different in-Rawdah dwell targets. Output is a policy brief with KPIs (peak holding occupancy, wait variance, share waiting over 30 minutes) and a live arrival-window feature that plugs into Direction 1. *يفوز لأن:* Gives the pitch measurable, evidence-based claims that most teams will not have, and extends published academic work with real Madinah timings. It costs nothing in hardware and can be built in weeks by a Python-strong team. It reframes the entry from an app to an operating model, which is what the Authority asked for. *قد يخسر لأن:* Simulation is not a product; the jury for a Culture and Values track wants something guests feel. Calibration from outside observation is partial (no access to inside corridor timings or women's areas without a female team member), and an over-confident number would violate the team's own honesty rule. The Springer 2025 paper already covers partitioning, so novelty must come from arrival-window and pre-verification policies.

**المخاطر:** No access to the Nusuk permit API or Authority batching data before the camp: every permit-time trigger and batch-status feed must be simulated and labelled as such, and the client may still read the entry as a Nusuk feature request rather than a pilotable field product. · Track mismatch: A1-1 sits under الثقافة والقيم, so a hardware or dashboard-heavy pitch (Direction 3 or 4 alone) risks being judged off-track; the guest-facing experience must lead. · Religious and etiquette content for the waiting feed needs Sharia and Authority review; LLM-generated or team-authored devotional text presented without governance could disqualify the idea on sanctity grounds. · Connectivity and battery failures in the courtyards (named in the brief under B2-3) break any phone-only channel; SMS and printed QR fallbacks must be real, not slides. · Women's flow is where the brief says stress is highest, but a male-majority team cannot observe women's holding areas; without a female team member or structured interviews the solution will be men's-side biased.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** Three minutes, no network dependency, Arabic slides with Western digits. 0:00 to 0:30, the problem in Madinah: two real photos taken by the team of a courtyard holding point, a 10-second clip of visitors asking where to go, and two quoted guest complaints from the brief. 0:30 to 1:30, the guest: a phone in Urdu or Indonesian receives the T-3h message with the mapped route from the central-area hotel to the correct gate and gathering point, then at the holding point the offline PWA shows batch 12 of 48, about 9 minutes, and the line that the slot is protected and there is no need to push; at T-10min it turns into a quiet preparation card. 1:30 to 2:15, the field staff: on a tablet the presenter taps '10 minutes remaining, please sit' and, in the same shot, three guest phones in three languages and a display page update silently with no sound. 2:15 to 2:45, the evidence: one chart from the SimPy twin, calibrated with the team's own stopwatch data, comparing burst arrival against staggered windows on peak holding occupancy and wait variance, with an honest confidence range. 2:45 to 3:00, the honesty slide: what is real (PWA, broadcaster, simulation, field data) and what is simulated (permit feed), plus a one-holding-point pilot plan with the Authority's walking teams. The jury should leave believing a pilot could start next month with the Authority's own staff and screens.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** Living in Madinah is the team's biggest asset for this challenge. Feasible within the rules: (1) walk the full journey themselves using المسار الفوري on several days and prayer times, logging each checkpoint, the number of permit checks, and timestamps from courtyard arrival to Rawdah exit; (2) stopwatch wait distributions and count QR-scan throughput per lane from public courtyard positions at different times to calibrate the simulation and catch the verification-speed variance the brief warns about; (3) photograph and map routes from the main central-area hotel clusters, the Haramain train shuttle drop-off and bus stands to the specific gates and gathering points, for real pre-arrival content; (4) record, by ear and notebook not audio inside the mosque, the phrases staff actually use at sorting points, to build the canonical instruction set and its translations; (5) short interviews with non-Arabic-speaking visitors outside the courtyards about what they did not understand and what they feared, in Urdu, Indonesian, Turkish and English via fellow students; (6) recruit a female team member or relatives to observe the women's holding areas and timings, which the brief flags as the highest-stress flow; (7) test mobile-data quality and PWA loading in the courtyards at peak so the offline design is proven, not assumed; (8) note the existing display screens, shade and seating at each holding point to design the kiosk mode and heat-aware content; (9) after the virtual camp, request an observation session with the Authority's walking teams to validate the instruction set. All photography must stay within permitted areas and the team should state clearly in the pitch which observations were made from outside the prayer halls.

### B2-2 — إدارة الأمتعة والأغراض الشخصية

**المشكلة في سطر:** Visitors arriving straight from trains and buses carry suitcases and bags that are refused at the doors or abandoned around courtyards, because storage points are few, limited to 4 hours and 7 kg, unknown to first-timers, and do not cover valuables, medicines or long worship stays.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** The goal is stated bluntly: zero luggage inside the arcades, mataf and courtyards, without creating pile-ups at outer points, and 'ease of application without comprehensive modifications'. The Authority wants an operating model and digital layer that intercepts luggage earlier in the journey (station, hotel, outer ring), gives visitors a trusted way to keep small valuables, tells every visitor in their language where to go before they reach a door, and smooths the after-prayer pickup peak. Security is a first-class axis, which suits a cybersecurity-led team.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Authority free luggage storage centres at the Grand Mosque (5 centres):** Free 24-hour storage with QR-based electronic tracking; conditions: Umrah permit shown via Nusuk, max 4 hours, bags only, max 7 kg, no valuables or prohibited items; located near the eastern and western courtyards, Ajyad and the third expansion services building. *لماذا لا يكفي:* The brief's own pain points: 4 hours is too short, valuables and medicines excluded, pile-ups at pickup, poor awareness among first-timers, and capacity not covering all approach points. — Okaz 'أربع ساعات مدة الانتظار في 5 مراكز'; SPA N2276018 and N2233298; alharamain.gov.sa service page; pep.gov.sa news 898
- **Prophet's Mosque electronic luggage lockers:** Paid electronic locker boxes at courtyard gates, plus a newer storage centre. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Paid, gate-specific and unadvertised; nothing links them to the guest's arrival route or to the free service model in Makkah. — SPA 1513725 and Al-Riyadh 1514146 (four locker centres at courtyard gates 1, 5, 18, 26, paid, electronic); Travel Yalla listing of a new centre between exits 365 and 366
- **SAR 'Hajj Without Baggage' and Haramain High Speed Railway cargo service:** Airport-to-accommodation luggage transport for Hajj pilgrims; cargo shipping for excess luggage at stations. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Hajj-season and airport-centric; the everyday Umrah visitor stepping off the train with a suitcase still has no hand-off before the mosque. — Gulf News on HHR carrying 2 million pilgrims and the second year of 'Hajj Without a Baggage'; sar.hhr.sa baggage guidelines

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** The pieces exist but are not one journey: no capacity visibility, no booking, no multilingual pre-arrival guidance, no bridge between station/hotel and the courtyard, no compliant way to keep a small valuables pouch, and no security tooling for abandoned bags. A unified 'luggage layer' (capacity map, digital tag, guidance, pickup slotting, abandoned-bag detection) is pure software plus light IoT, fits Nusuk's pattern, and can be prototyped in Madinah where the team can walk the whole route.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **أمانة: طبقة موحّدة لحفظ الأمتعة عبر نُسك** (Amanah: unified luggage layer inside the Nusuk journey): A Nusuk-style service where the visitor, before leaving the train or hotel, sees live capacity of storage points near their intended gate in their language, reserves a slot, gets a digital tag (QR plus optional NFC sticker) and step-by-step directions; drop-off and pickup are scan-based, pickup is slotted after prayer to avoid pile-ups, and stays beyond 4 hours are extended in-app under rules the Authority sets. Deaf/blind support via large icons, haptics and audio. *يفوز لأن:* Fixes the three loudest pain points (awareness, distance refusal, pickup queues) without physical works; integrates with the Authority's existing QR tracking; security design is a differentiator. *قد يخسر لأن:* Depends on Nusuk integration that the team can only mock; the Authority may consider it an incremental upgrade of a service they already run.
- **محطة الاعتراض: تسليم الأمتعة عند القطار والفندق** (Intercept Station: luggage hand-off at train and hotel): An operating model where luggage is intercepted before the central area: counters at the Haramain station and partner hotels accept bags, a tagged-bag logistics run moves them to the visitor's hotel or to a peripheral pickup hub, and the visitor walks to the mosque empty-handed with only a small compliant pouch. The software handles chain-of-custody, sealed-bag scans at every hop, driver manifests and exception alerts; the field model borrows from SAR's Hajj Without Baggage. *يفوز لأن:* Attacks the root cause (bags arriving at the door at all) and speaks to the brief's 'integrated logistics supply chain'; the custody ledger answers security questions convincingly. *قد يخسر لأن:* Requires multi-party agreements (SAR, hotels, the Authority) the team cannot secure; a jury may judge it as a policy proposal with an app attached.
- **عين الساحة: رصد الأمتعة المتروكة وربطها بالمفقودات** (Courtyard Eye: abandoned-luggage detection linked to lost-and-found): Edge computer vision on courtyard cameras flags stationary unattended bags near pillars and toilet exits, alerts security with location and dwell time, and when a bag is collected it is auto-registered in lost-and-found with a photo so the owner can claim it by description at a care centre; privacy-preserving (no face retention). Uses the same lost-and-found channel the care centres already run. *يفوز لأن:* Targets the security and 'easier surveillance without visual distractions' axes directly and is the kind of thing a cybersecurity team is expected to build well. *قد يخسر لأن:* Camera access inside the mosque grounds will not be granted for a prototype; training data must be staged; it manages the symptom (abandoned bags) rather than the journey.

**المخاطر:** The Authority already operates the storage service and its QR tracking; the entry must show a clearly larger journey, not a nicer front end. · Nusuk and SAR integrations can only be mocked; the jury will ask who owns the tags and the data. · Valuables and medicines are explicitly excluded from current storage; any 'keep your pouch' proposal needs a security policy the team cannot set alone. · Pickup surges after prayer are a physical capacity issue; slotting software helps only if counters have enough staff.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** A judge plays a first-time visitor arriving at the Madinah train station: on a phone in Urdu they see live capacity at Gate 5 lockers, reserve, and receive a tag and walking directions; at a mock counter a second judge scans the tag, the bag is 'stored', and the ops screen shows counter load. The audience triggers 300 simulated pickups after prayer and the slotting engine spreads them across three counters with waiting times shown. Finally a short clip of a bag left by a courtyard pillar: the detector flags it after 90 seconds and it lands in the lost-and-found queue with a photo. All offline on one laptop.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** Excellent: walk the route from the Madinah Haramain station and bus drop-offs to the Prophet's Mosque with a suitcase, photograph where bags are refused and where they pile up around pillars and toilet exits, map and time the existing locker centres at gates 1, 5, 18, 26 and the newer centre, observe post-prayer pickup queues, and interview arriving visitors and hotel receptionists. This gives real photos and numbers for the pitch that Makkah-only teams cannot easily produce.

### A1-2 — التجربة الإيمانية والتاريخية داخل الروضة الشريفة بلغات متعددة مع الحفاظ على القدسية والسكينة

**المشكلة في سطر:** In the 10-15 minutes (sometimes under 10, per guest voice in the brief) that a batched visitor gets inside the ≤330 m² Rawdah, they pray and greet the Prophet ﷺ surrounded by pillars, mihrabs and the Sacred Chamber they cannot name, receive instructions in a language they do not understand, and are moved out without ever grasping what they stood beside in a once-in-a-lifetime moment.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** Reading the priority axes ("balance deepening the experience vs. batching efficiency"; "the guest's urgent need to know where they stand and what they see") together with the operational factor ("enrichment must be condensed, fast-acting and precisely synchronised with the guest's stay"), the Authority is not asking for more content: it already publishes pillar histories on its own site, runs the دليل المصلي QR platform in 7 languages, deploys Manara robots in 11 languages and smart screens in 50+ languages. What it lacks is DELIVERY at the right second, in the right language, without adding one second to the batch or one visible/audible device inside the Rawdah. It wants (1) enrichment mostly moved out of the Rawdah into the ~20-minute wait and the post-visit, leaving only orientation ("you are standing at أسطوانة التوبة") inside; (2) something that plugs into what it already owns (Nusuk permit time + language, the holding points, its own field staff) rather than a standalone product; (3) a reduction in the staff-pushing pain point, i.e. the guest should already know the sequence and the remaining time so staff do not have to shout; (4) proof that the solution is sanctity-safe enough to pilot: no speakers, no screens, no lights, no lingering. The unstated fear is that any enrichment tool will make people stop, film and block the flow; a winning entry must show it does the opposite.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Nusuk app (نسك) - Rawdah permits and 'Ziyarah locations' info:** Issues the free Rawdah permit (time slot, gender, personal), supports Arabic, English, French, Turkish, Urdu, Malay, Persian per search summaries; includes a generic 'Ziyarah locations' guided-information section. *لماذا لا يكفي:* It knows the guest's slot and language but does nothing with them at the moment of the visit: no stage-by-stage orientation, no pillar-level content, no synchronisation with batching. Guest voice in the brief shows it is already a friction point (gender mis-booking, waiting lists), so guests will not explore it for enrichment. — https://saudipedia.com/en/nusuk-app ; https://blog.wego.com/rawdah-permit-guide/
- **دليل المصلي (Musalli Guide) - the Authority's QR platform in 7 languages:** Interactive multilingual (Arabic, English, Indonesian, Malay, French, Turkish, Urdu) guidance/awareness content reached by scanning QR codes placed around the Two Holy Mosques. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Generic awareness content, not Rawdah-specific spatial orientation; scanning a QR code while moving in a packed batch is impractical and would itself stop the flow; 7 languages leaves out Bengali, Hausa, Chinese, Russian and others that the Manara robot already covers; nothing is timed to the permit. — https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2520069 ; https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6680020
- **المساعد الإثرائي الذكي (Smart Enrichment Assistant) - Agency for Religious Affairs, Prophet's Mosque:** AI-based enrichment app for pilgrims and visitors, launched 18 May 2025 (first version). Feature details beyond the announcement are unverified from the search summary. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Announced as a general AI enrichment/Q&A assistant, not as a Rawdah-batch companion; nothing indicates on-site, offline, silent or stage-synchronised delivery. Any team proposal must position itself as a module this assistant or Nusuk could adopt, not as a competitor. — https://ksa.com/prophets-mosque-launches-ai-powered-smart-enrichment-assistant/
- **Manara / Manarat al-Haramain guidance robot (Manara 2):** AI robot by Duraya and SWB deployed by the Presidency of Religious Affairs in both mosques; 11 languages (Arabic, English, French, Russian, Persian, Turkish, Malay, Urdu, Chinese, Bengali, Hausa); introduces locations, answers religious questions, instant translation. *لماذا لا يكفي:* A stationary physical robot cannot enter the Rawdah or serve 48,000 guests/day in 10-minute slots; it is exactly the kind of visible, attention-grabbing device the challenge rules out inside. Useful as proof that 11 languages is the Authority's baseline. — https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/661314 ; https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-deploys-ai-powered-multilingual-robot-for-pilgrims-at-two-holy-mosques-1.500540029
- **40 multilingual smart screens (Ramadan 2026) and 50+ language guidance services:** Screens broadcasting verified rulings and guidance in 50+ languages at key locations; human spatial and cultural guidance services in 50+ languages. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Screens inside the Rawdah would violate the sanctity constraint and cannot be personalised to one guest's language; human guides cannot scale to every batch and their instructions are the 'psychological pressure' pain point named in the brief. — https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/ramadan-2026-40-multilingual-smart-screens-rolled-out-at-two-holy-mosques-1.500452710 ; https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/5/18/

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** Every existing layer (Nusuk, Musalli Guide, Smart Enrichment Assistant, robots, screens, VR tour) delivers content either before the trip or at fixed points outside the Rawdah, in the Authority's chosen languages, with no relation to the batch the guest is actually in. Nobody has built the 'last 30 minutes': the holding-point wait (20 min average per the brief) as a structured mental-map preparation, a silent glance-level orientation inside (which pillar, which mihrab, where the Chamber is, how much time remains), and a post-exit keepsake, all keyed to the permit's time slot and language. Concretely: (1) no stage-sequenced content (arrive - hold - enter - pray - greet - exit) exists in any language; (2) no solution answers 'where do I stand' without a screen or a person; (3) languages beyond the Authority's 7-11 (Bengali, Hausa, Amharic, Tamil, Sinhala, Uzbek, Fulani) are unserved even though those guests are the least able to read Arabic plaques; (4) the staff-side has no multilingual way to say 'two minutes left, please move calmly toward the Chamber' without shouting; (5) nothing measures whether enrichment adds seconds to the batch, which is the Authority's real KPI. The opportunity is a sanctity-first delivery layer that the Authority can bolt onto Nusuk/Musalli Guide: content stays theirs, timing and language targeting are the innovation, and the demo must prove zero added dwell time.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **سكينة — رفيق الروضة المتزامن مع التصريح** (Sakinah - permit-synchronised silent Rawdah companion): An offline-first web app (single HTML, no store install, opened from a Nusuk-style link or QR at the holding point) keyed to the permit time and the guest's language. Three stages: T-3h briefing in the mother tongue (what the Rawdah is, etiquette, a 60-second numbered map of the 8 pillars, the two mihrabs, the minbar and the Sacred Chamber); at the holding point a 'calm preparation' sequence (one-line story per pillar, the du'a of greeting, breathing/waiting cues instead of anxious silence); inside, a 'glance mode': screen dimmed, camera locked out, at most 3 lines per stage (pray - greet - exit) advanced by a single haptic pulse, no sound; after exit, a keepsake card 'you stood beside أسطوانة التوبة' with the du'as and a share-safe image (no photos of the interior). Content is drawn only from the Authority's published pillar narratives. *يفوز لأن:* It answers the priority axis literally: roughly 80% of the enrichment is moved into the wait and the post-visit, only orientation stays inside, and the demo can show 0 seconds added to batch time and 0 devices installed. It plugs into assets the Authority already owns (permit time, language, holding points) and the team has shipped this exact stack (Rafiq: FastAPI, offline Arabic UI, scope-guarded agents). Field-testable in Madinah with real permits. *قد يخسر لأن:* 'Another app' reflex from the jury, and the visible use of a phone inside the Rawdah conflicts with the no-photography etiquette and with the image of a person absorbed in prayer; without a real Nusuk API the synchronisation is simulated by a link/QR and the jury may see that as vaporware; the Authority could reply that Musalli Guide or the Smart Enrichment Assistant can absorb this as a feature, which is only a win if the team frames it as that module from the start.
- **أساطين — طبقة تعريف صامتة عند الأعمدة** (Asateen - silent proximity layer at the pillars): Coin-sized off-the-shelf BLE beacons (no light, no sound, no screen) fixed discreetly near each of the 8 pillars and at the Sacred Chamber approach; the guest's phone (same PWA as Sakinah or a thin native shell) vibrates once and shows the pillar's name and a one-line story in the guest's language when within about 3 m. Optional tiny numbered markers at pillar bases matching the printed/digital Rawdah card handed at the holding point, so even a guest with no phone can match number 4 to 'أسطوانة السرير'. The team's security angle: spoof-resistant beacon IDs, signed payloads, and an audit log, so no one can hijack beacons to push unofficial content. *يفوز لأن:* It is the only direction that truly answers 'where do I stand and what do I see' at the exact spot, with nothing visible or audible; the physical marker fallback serves low-tech guests; the IoT-security story (verified content chain in a sacred space) is unique to a cybersecurity-led team and differentiates it from every content app. *قد يخسر لأن:* Web Bluetooth is not supported on iOS Safari, forcing a native app; human bodies attenuate 2.4 GHz and the pillars are only a few metres apart (spacing is an assumption to verify on site), so 3 m accuracy in a packed batch is unreliable and false triggers would confuse guests; installing anything inside the Rawdah needs Authority approval the team cannot get before the camp; battery maintenance; a jury may still read beacons as 'tech inside the Rawdah'.
- **همس — سرد صوتي موقوت بالتفويج عبر سماعة الزائر** (Hams - batch-timed whisper narration through the guest's own earbud): At the holding point the guest selects a language and pre-downloads a 4-minute narration; the batch supervisor taps 'batch entering' on a small console; every phone in that batch starts the same sequenced whisper (entering the green carpet, the pillar on your right, prayer time, walking to the Chamber, greeting, exit) through the guest's own single earbud, silent between cues, followed by a 2-minute reflection track after exit. If pursued, restrict audio to before entry and after exit and keep the inside purely silent, which collapses it into direction 1.
- **مرشد الروضة — وكيل محادثة بلا تثبيت وبطاقات عبارات للمرشدين** (Murshid al-Rawdah - no-install multilingual agent plus staff phrase cards): A WhatsApp/Telegram bot (no app install, works on any phone) that answers in 30+ languages only from an Authority-approved corpus about the Rawdah's pillars, mihrabs, Chamber, etiquette and the visit sequence, before and after the visit; paired with a staff-side card deck (paper and tablet) of polite, calm instructions ('2 minutes left, please move toward the Chamber', 'this is the Pillar of Repentance') rendered in the batch's top languages, replacing shouting and pushing. Out-of-corpus questions are refused and logged, never answered from model memory. *يفوز لأن:* Directly targets the 'psychological pressure from staff' pain point and the language barrier with zero adoption friction; plays to the team's proven strength (guard-railed Claude agents); the Authority keeps full editorial control through the corpus; cheap to pilot at one holding point. *قد يخسر لأن:* Overlaps the Presidency's Smart Enrichment Assistant and the Manara robot, so the jury may ask 'why not a feature there'; the fear of AI hallucination on religious and historical facts is strong with this client, and one wrong answer in a demo ends the pitch; nothing happens inside the Rawdah itself, so it may be judged as an A1-1 (waiting) entry; WhatsApp Business approval and cost.

**المخاطر:** Phone use inside the Rawdah: the Authority discourages photography there (team observation to confirm on site), so any in-Rawdah screen element can be read as encouraging phones; mitigate with camera lock-out, dimmed 3-line glance cards, and by moving most content to the holding point. · Religious and historical accuracy: pillar names, counts (Authority says 6 famous, Al-Watan says 8) and stories must come verbatim from Authority-approved sources with scholar review; no LLM-generated facts, and translations of religious terms must be human-checked for at least the top 10 languages. · No Nusuk or permit API access: synchronisation must be simulated via a link/QR or manual 'I entered' tap; the pitch must state this honestly (the team's own rule) and show the integration path rather than claim it. · Crowding out by the Authority's own products (Musalli Guide, Smart Enrichment Assistant, Manara, smart screens): the entry must be framed as the missing 'last 30 minutes' module that these can adopt, otherwise the jury asks why it is not just a feature. · Track ambiguity with A1-1: if the demo emphasises the waiting area, judges may move it mentally to the waiting challenge; keep the inside-the-Rawdah orientation and the post-visit keepsake as the visible core.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** Three minutes, entirely offline (airplane mode shown on screen at the start, per the team's rule). 0:00-0:30: a Bengali-speaking persona (representing the languages the Authority's 7-language platform does not cover) receives a Nusuk-style message 3 hours before her permit; one tap opens a 60-second card in Bengali with the numbered map of the Rawdah, the 8 pillars, the two mihrabs, the minbar and the Sacred Chamber. 0:30-1:15: at the holding point (real photo the team took in the courtyard with permission, plus one real visitor quote from field interviews) the screen shows the 'calm preparation' sequence: one line per pillar, the greeting du'a in Bengali with Arabic audio-free transliteration, and a remaining-stage indicator instead of a clock. 1:15-2:00: the presenter says 'entering' and the phone drops into glance mode: dimmed, camera disabled, three lines only ('You are at Ustuwanat al-Tawba. Pray here. Next: walk right toward the Chamber'), advanced by a single vibration; the presenter never looks at the phone for more than two seconds, demonstrating zero added dwell time. 2:00-2:30: exit card: 'You stood beside three pillars and greeted the Prophet ﷺ', du'as, a share-safe illustrated keepsake with no interior photos. 2:30-3:00: the staff console shows the batch's language mix and the one-tap polite multilingual instruction cards, then a closing slide with honest metrics: content in 12 reviewed languages, 0 sound, 0 screens installed, 0 seconds added per batch, tested on site at the holding points on N days, integration path via Nusuk link or Musalli Guide QR, and a clear 'what is real vs simulated' box.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** Living in Madinah is the decisive edge for this challenge and the team should use it before the idea deadline and throughout the camp: (1) map the actual holding points (نقاط الفرز) for men and women, count batches per hour, time the wait and the inside stay with a stopwatch over several days and prayer times, and record how staff give instructions and in which languages; (2) photograph (where permitted, outside the Rawdah) the existing signage and check whether the pillars carry readable name plaques and in which languages, which decides how much in-Rawdah orientation is even needed; (3) measure mobile data and Wi-Fi strength in the holding corridors and courtyards to justify the offline-first design; (4) interview 20-30 visitors from South Asia, Indonesia, Turkey, West Africa and Central Asia at the courtyards after their visit with a 5-question script ('did you know which pillar you prayed at?', 'what did staff say and did you understand?') to produce real quotes and a language-need table; (5) use each team member's one annual permit (and the 'immediate path' if available) to run a real end-to-end test of the glance mode, spacing the tests across the prototype phase; (6) test the PWA and the staff cards at a holding point with a few consenting visitors, and time whether it changes dwell; (7) locate where Musalli Guide QR codes and Manara robots actually stand relative to the Rawdah route, to argue the gap with photos; (8) bench-test BLE reception in a dense crowd in the courtyards (not inside) if the beacon direction is kept; (9) approach the Authority's field guides and the Madinah-side staff informally to learn the batching rules and the language mix by shift; (10) verify the pillar count and names against the Authority's own page and a scholar at the mosque before any content goes into the prototype.

### C1-1 — تحسين الاستجابة للحالات الطارئة والإنسانية داخل الحرمين بالذكاء الاصطناعي

**المشكلة في سطر:** In packed crowds a fainting or collapse is reported late and imprecisely, responders cannot penetrate the mass, and language barriers slow triage; the Authority wants digital and field proactivity that cuts minutes without confusing crowds or violating sanctity.

- **Authority interactive maps roadmap (2026):** Plans to link the interactive maps and metric addressing to smart emergency alert systems and to develop route apps. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Announced direction, not a delivered incident-reporting layer; this is where a team prototype could plug in. — Youm7 / Sabq Feb 2026

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** The uncovered layer is the first 90 seconds: bystander-initiated, language-free reporting with a machine-readable location (the Authority's new metric addressing and 650+ QR points give an anchor), plus responder routing through the crowd and a silent way to alert nearby field staff. ### B1-3 — التعامل الأمثل مع طيور الحرم

**المشكلة في سطر:** Emotionally driven random feeding keeps huge pigeon flocks in the central area, courtyards and arcades, whose acidic droppings corrode marble and gilding, soil worshippers' clothes and consume large daily cleaning effort.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** The Authority does not want the pigeons gone; it wants their behaviour relocated. The wording ('gentle, non-harmful, non-repellent', 'attract them to safe designated areas at the edges', 'multilingual awareness that cleanliness does not contradict honouring the pigeons') tells you the real ask is a behavioural system for humans first and birds second: control where food appears, make the designated zones the attractive ones, measure the result, and do it without ever being seen as hostile to birds that many pilgrims regard as blessed.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Makkah Municipality 'ليه؟' awareness campaign and feeding fines:** Municipal awareness campaign against random feeding in public places, reported fines for feeding in non-designated spots, and reported automated monitoring of grain sellers. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Municipality jurisdiction stops at the mosque boundary; enforcement-led messaging conflicts with pilgrims' reverence for the birds and the brief explicitly wants gentle steering, not fines. — alkhalij24 / news50.sa on the campaign and sign removal; Alwatan 'الرصد الآلي هل يمنع بيع حبوب الحمام'; X post citing a 1,000 SAR fine (fine amount press-reported, not verified on hmm.gov.sa)
- **Trafalgar Square food-reduction programme and Venice St Mark's feeding ban:** Controlled reduction of available food cut Trafalgar Square's flock from roughly 4,000 to about 200 without culling; Venice banned feeding with fines. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Both are secular-city bans with no designated feeding alternative and no multilingual, faith-sensitive framing; neither had to manage 24-hour crowds of millions. — Wikipedia 'Save the Trafalgar Square Pigeons'; NBC News on Venice
- **Contraceptive feed (OvoControl) and dovecote schemes:** Nicarbazin-laced feed at fixed feeders reduces hatch rates (vendor claims ~50%/year decline); European cities use managed dovecotes to concentrate birds and remove eggs. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Interfering with Haram pigeons' eggs or reproduction is religiously and publicly sensitive (fatwa literature treats Haram game as protected), and neither is an awareness or spatial-steering system. — ovocontrol.com; animalpeopleforum.org
- **Daily washing and cleaning by the Authority's sanitation teams:** Manual, water-intensive cleaning of droppings from marble and facades. *لماذا لا يكفي:* The brief calls this the huge daily waste of water, materials and effort that a solution should cut. — Brief B1-3 priorities; Makkah municipality press on continuous washing

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** Nobody has instrumented the problem: where the flocks concentrate, when feeding happens, which spots get soiled fastest, and whether designated feeding zones actually shift the birds. A software team can build the measurement and nudging layer (vision-based flock/droppings mapping, timed feeders at edge zones, multilingual sadaqah-style feeding channel) and leave bird biology to the Authority's environmental partners. Innovation room is high because almost no technology entrants will pick a pigeon challenge.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **مرصد الطيور: خريطة حرارية للأسراب والتلوث** (Bird Observatory: flock and soiling heat-map): Edge computer vision on existing or cheap cameras counts pigeons per zone per minute and flags fresh droppings on marble, producing a live heat-map of concentration and soiling. Cleaning crews get a priority list (clean here first, this zone soils every day at 16:00), and management gets before/after evidence for any intervention. Runs on-device, no faces stored, respecting privacy and sanctity. *يفوز لأن:* Turns an emotional argument into measured data; directly targets the 'cut daily waste of water and effort' priority with a scheduling gain; the team can train on its own Madinah footage. *قد يخسر لأن:* Measuring is not solving; a jury may say the flock is still in the arcade. Camera placement inside the mosque needs the Authority's consent.
- **مقاصد الحمام: محطات إطعام موقوتة في الأطراف** (Pigeon Havens: timed feeding stations at courtyard edges): Designated feeding plazas outside the operational core with automated timed grain dispensers (fed from licensed grain vendors), water trays and shade, so the flock learns that food appears only there and only at set times. Visitors are invited by multilingual signage and QR to feed at the stations, keeping the honoured tradition. The controller logs dispense times and integrates with the Observatory to prove the flock shift. *يفوز لأن:* This is the literal 'attract them to safe designated areas' the brief asks for, and animal-behaviour literature supports food-place conditioning; the stations are gentle and visibly respectful. *قد يخسر لأن:* Requires municipal and Authority coordination on land outside the boundary; results take weeks of conditioning to show, longer than a pitch cycle; vendor economics (grain sellers) push back.
- **إطعام بنيّة: قناة رقمية للصدقة على طيور الحرم** (Feed with Intention: a digital channel for pigeon-feeding charity): A multilingual, Nusuk-compatible page (and QR on signs) that lets a visitor 'feed the Haram pigeons' by sponsoring grain delivered to the edge stations, with a short faith-framed explanation that cleanliness honours the place. The visitor gets a certificate and the station's live counter shows their contribution being dispensed. Turns the emotional impulse into a channel that removes grain from the arcades. *يفوز لأن:* Fits the Authority's proven Ihsan donation pattern for iftar, is fully software, multilingual and demoable, and addresses the human factor the brief identifies as the root cause. *قد يخسر لأن:* Seen as gimmicky or as monetising a religious act; a jury may ask what happens to the physical bags of grain sold on the street today.

**المخاطر:** Religious and public sensitivity: any perception of harming or hunger-controlling Haram pigeons would be reputationally fatal; the pitch must be explicit that no bird is harmed. · Behaviour change in birds takes weeks; the prototype can prove detection and station mechanics but not the flock shift within the programme window. · Jurisdiction split between the Authority (inside) and the municipality (central area streets and grain vendors). · Camera-based detection inside the mosque must be justified against sanctity and privacy concerns; keep it to courtyards and edges.

### C1-2 — التخصيص الفائق للخدمات حسب الفئة والزمان والمكان والكثافة

**المشكلة في سطر:** Carts, Zamzam, guidance and mushaf distribution run on fixed schedules and locations regardless of who is actually in each zone at each hour; the Authority wants a system that senses the dominant segment per zone, predicts needs and re-routes resources and staff skills automatically.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **SDAIA Baseer / Sawaher density analytics and GeoAI:** Real-time crowd density and congestion detection from video, thermal and GeoAI for crowd management during Hajj and in the Grand Mosque. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Measures how many, not who (age, mobility, language) and does not translate density into service allocation decisions; access is limited to security bodies. — Asharq Al-Awsat, SPA N2334843, Al Arabiya May 2026

- **Interactive maps with routes optimised by operational variables:** 100,000+ dynamic routes recomputed against operational variables and crowd movement. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Guest-facing routing; it does not move carts, staff or Zamzam supply to where the segment mix demands. — Sabq / Youm7 Feb 2026
- **Academic crowd density classification:** ML models classifying density levels from Hajj video frames. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Research on density only; no demographic segmentation or service demand forecasting found in search results. — arXiv 2501.04911 (87% accuracy on KAU HAJJv2), PMC9044363

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** No evidence of any deployed or published system that forecasts demand per segment per zone per hour and recommends redeployment of carts, Zamzam, guides and staff skills. The team can build an explainable engine (deterministic rules plus light forecasting) on public prayer times, weather and a simulated or opt-in density feed, and make the 'why' visible, which is the style of engine the team already ships. The organisational sub-problem (skill-to-shift matching) is almost pure software and has no incumbent.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **نسيج** (Naseej: context-aware allocation engine): A decision-support engine that ingests zone occupancy (simulated, or an opt-in feed), prayer times, weather, calendar (Ramadan, weekends, school holidays) and historical service logs, infers the dominant segment per zone per hour (elderly mid-morning, youth at tahajjud) and recommends moving carts, Zamzam replenishment, mushaf trolleys and multilingual guides, each recommendation with an explanation and expected effect. Operators accept or reject, and the log trains the model. *يفوز لأن:* Matches the challenge text almost word for word; explainability answers the jury's trust question; demoable end-to-end with a replayed day; no hardware. *قد يخسر لأن:* Runs on synthetic or assumed data until the Authority shares logs; a jury may ask 'how do you know elderly dominate at 10 am' and the honest answer is 'assumption plus observation at the Prophet's Mosque'; can look like a generic ops dashboard.
- **مناوبة ذكية** (Smart Shift: skills-to-segment rostering): A staffing tool that matches field staff and volunteers (languages, elderly-care training, sign language) to predicted incoming segments per gate and shift, produces a shift brief in each worker's language, and reallocates during the shift when the mix changes. Uses the same forecasting layer as Naseej but targets the organisational factor named in the brief. *يفوز لأن:* Narrow, tractable, directly addresses the 'organisational' factor few teams will pick; measurable KPI (language match rate at gate level); pilotable with a single volunteer group at the Prophet's Mosque. *قد يخسر لأن:* Less visually exciting; the AI content is thin unless the forecasting layer is strong; requires HR data the team will have to fabricate for the demo.
- **خدمة لي** (Service for Me: opt-in guest profile as demand signal): Guests optionally declare mobility, age band and language when they take a permit or scan a QR at a gate; they get a personalised route (nearest elderly prayer area, cart pickup with lowest estimated wait, Zamzam point, guide in their language). Aggregated, anonymised profiles per zone become the live segment signal the operations engine uses, replacing camera-based demographic inference that is privacy-sensitive. *يفوز لأن:* Gives the jury a guest-facing demo and an operations demo from one data flow; sidesteps the ethical problem of inferring age or nationality from video; leverages the team's security and privacy credibility. *قد يخسر لأن:* Adoption problem: only a fraction of guests will opt in, so the signal is biased; depends on Nusuk or gate-QR integration; the personalised guidance overlaps with the Authority's interactive maps.

**المخاطر:** Data access is the core risk: without service logs and occupancy data the engine is a well-argued simulation. · Demographic inference from cameras is a privacy and dignity minefield; the team must avoid it explicitly or be attacked in Q&A. · The Authority's own analytics partners (SDAIA) may be perceived as the natural owner of this problem. · A dashboard-heavy demo is harder to make emotionally compelling in three minutes than a guest-facing one.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** Minute 1: replay a synthetic Ramadan day on the zone map of the Prophet's Mosque; at 10:00 the engine flags 'elderly-dominant, courtyard east, cart demand +40%' and recommends moving 12 carts and 2 Urdu-speaking guides, with the reasoning shown in Arabic. Minute 2: an operator accepts; a supervisor's phone shows the reallocation; at 01:30 the mix flips to youth at tahajjud and Zamzam replenishment moves instead. Minute 3: show the opt-in guest PWA giving an elderly visitor a route to the nearest designated prayer area, then the KPI panel (estimated wait reduction, service-hours saved) with the disclaimer that inputs are simulated and observed, not Authority data.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** The team can run structured observation at the Prophet's Mosque across the day (sunrise, mid-morning, after asr, taraweeh in Ramadan, tahajjud), counting apparent age mix and mobility aids at specific gates and courtyards, noting cart queue lengths and Zamzam refill times, and interviewing cart-service staff about when demand peaks. This gives real, cited observations to replace pure assumptions in the model and in the pitch.

### B1-2 — حواجز إدارة الحشود المناسبة لخصوصية الحرمين

**المشكلة في سطر:** The Haramain rely on plastic and heavy metal barriers that choke turns, trip people on their bases, look out of place, are hard to clean and cannot be reshaped in minutes when crowd density shifts.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** The Authority wants an industrial-design product: a lightweight, nestable, anti-climb, shock-absorbing barrier with integrated colour-changing guidance lighting (they literally specify green open / red closed), deployable without noise, wind-stable in open courtyards. The priority axis about opening and closing paths 'extremely fast on live density changes' means they also want the barrier to be part of a command system, not just furniture. A team that only offers the command system will be asked 'and the barrier?'.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Commercial modular event barriers (Mojo Barriers, Tensabarrier, Milos):** High-strength aluminium/steel crowd barriers, retractable-belt queue systems, foldable barriers; some vendors now integrate LED lighting and branding panels. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Designed for concerts and airports: visually industrial, still have protruding feet, no sanctity-aware aesthetic, and lighting is not tied to a live crowd-density control system. — mojorental.com; tensabarrier.com; milossystems.com; industry summaries noting LED and RFID integration as emerging standard
- **Smart counting sensors and AI camera platforms in the Grand Mosque (Sawaher, Baseer, SMART MOC):** Real-time density monitoring at gates and corridors with sensors and multi-camera AI, feeding operations centres. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Sensing exists, but the last mile, physically re-routing the crowd, is still done by staff dragging barriers; there is no documented link from density alerts to barrier state or guidance lighting. — Arab News 'Smart systems streamline crowds at Grand Mosque'; Asharq Al-Awsat on SDAIA Baseer; SPA N2334059
- **Current Authority practice in the Rawdah and courtyards (rope, plastic and metal barriers, staff hand signals):** Fixed and semi-fixed barriers plus manual batching. *لماذا لا يكفي:* The brief itself lists the failures: footprint, tripping feet, ugliness, dirt accumulation, slow reconfiguration. — Brief B1-2 and A1-1 descriptions

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** The credible software/IoT slice is the 'nervous system' of the barrier: a tamper-resistant lighting and state module that any barrier body can carry, controlled from a field tablet and (later) by density feeds, plus a layout planner that lets a supervisor redraw a corridor and get deployment instructions in minutes. The barrier body itself needs an industrial-design partner; without one the entry is half a product.

**اتجاهات الحل:**
- **نبض الحاجز: وحدة إضاءة وحالة ذكية لأي حاجز** (Barrier Pulse: smart status-lighting module for any barrier): A clip-on, battery-powered, sealed LED strip and radio node (ESP32, LoRa or BLE mesh) that gives any barrier a state: green open, red closed, amber slow, with soft diffused light visible from afar but not dazzling. Staff switch corridors from a tablet; the state is logged and signed (HMAC audit chain) so control rooms know exactly when each path changed. Fail-safe: loss of signal falls back to the last commanded state. *يفوز لأن:* Delivers the exact 'smart interactive lighting' the brief specifies, is hardware the team can actually build, and its security/audit story is unusual for a hackathon. *قد يخسر لأن:* It does not solve footprint, tripping feet, shock absorption or cleaning; a jury may see a nice accessory attached to the same ugly barrier.
- **مخطط المسارات الميداني وتوأم الحواجز الرقمي** (Field corridor planner and barrier digital twin): A tablet app where a field supervisor draws the desired corridors on a map of the courtyard or hall; the app computes which barrier segments to move, in what order, with a printable or spoken checklist per team, simulates flow with a simple pedestrian model, and records the resulting layout. Later integrates with density feeds to propose reconfigurations. *يفوز لأن:* Attacks the 'reshape in minutes' priority from the coordination side, which is where minutes are actually lost; demoable without any hardware. *قد يخسر لأن:* Pure software in a physical-product track; crowd simulation credibility is hard to establish without real density data.
- **حاجز خفيف متداخل بقاعدة غاطسة ومصنّع محلياً** (Lightweight nestable barrier with flush base (design partnership)): The full product answer: a recyclable composite or aluminium barrier with a flat weighted base (no protruding feet), vertical nesting, integrated soft anti-climb top, subtle cream/gold finish matching the mosque palette, with the Barrier Pulse module built in. Requires an industrial-design student and access to a fabrication lab; the software team supplies the electronics and control layer. *يفوز لأن:* This is what the Authority actually pictured; a physical mock-up with lighting and a control tablet would be a complete story. *قد يخسر لأن:* The team has no fabrication lab and no industrial designer; wind-load, shock and durability testing cannot be done credibly in four months by students.

**المخاطر:** Physical product expectations dominate; without a design partner the entry looks like an accessory. · Lighting inside prayer halls must be subtle; any colour-strobe demo will be read as violating sanctity. · Radio devices in the mosque require the Authority's security clearance; a jury may ask about jamming and spoofing, which the team can answer but must prepare for. · Barrier procurement is a large-contract decision; a student prototype has a long path to adoption.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** On stage, a 1-metre mock barrier segment with the Pulse strip. **البحث الميداني الممكن:** High for observation: photograph and measure barrier types, feet and gaps at the Prophet's Mosque courtyards and Rawdah holding points, time how long staff take to move a line at batch changes, count near-trips at peak exits, and interview field staff informally. No on-site hardware testing is possible without permission.

### B1-1 — التظليل وانعكاس الحرارة للأرضيات في الساحات والأسطح

**المشكلة في سطر:** Courtyards and roofs of both mosques expose barefoot crowds to direct sun and heat-storing floors; existing umbrellas do not cover transit routes from stations and tunnels, and worshippers crowd into whatever shade exists.

**ما تريده الهيئة فعلاً:** Reading the priority axes ('no effect on crowd flow or visual identity', 'adapt instantly to sudden weather changes', 'structural safety'), the Authority is asking primarily for an architectural/materials product: adaptive shading plus a floor treatment that lowers surface temperature and glare, without changing the white Thassos-marble look. The pain points about transit gaps and shade overcrowding hint at a secondary operational need: knowing where the unshaded, hottest paths are and steering people and shade resources there. The jury for this track will likely be engineers and facilities staff who think in kelvin and wind loads, not app screens.

**ما هو قائم:**
- **Thassos cool white marble flooring (both mosques):** Dolomitic Thassos marble imported since the late 1970s, laid as ~5 cm slabs over insulating/air-permeable layers; a peer-reviewed study confirms it keeps barefoot walking safe under Makkah climate data. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Brief still reports 'extreme hardship walking barefoot at noon' and glare for the elderly, so the marble alone does not solve peak-hour comfort on all surfaces (roofs, transit paths, non-marble areas). — Arab News 'Why the marble flooring of the Two Holy Mosques remains cool'; Construction and Building Materials 2021 study 'Cool white marble pavement thermophysical assessment at Al Masjid Al-Haram'
- **250 movable umbrellas at the Prophet's Mosque + mist fans:** Custom-designed retractable umbrellas covering 228,000+ worshippers at once, wind/fire/rain rated, plus mist fans and central cooling in the halls. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Coverage is static and confined to the main courtyards; the brief's pain point is the unshaded walk from transport stations and tunnels to the doors, and overcrowding under the shade that does exist. — SPA N2071197; alharamain.gov.sa umbrellas page; Sabq/Al-Jazirah Sep 2026 on the integrated cooling system
- **IoT heat-illness early-warning research for Hajj / wearable health bands:** Academic and pilot systems that monitor high-risk pilgrims' vitals and ambient heat and alert medical teams. *لماذا لا يكفي:* Person-centric and medical; nothing publicly documented maps surface temperature and shade availability across the courtyards to guide flow or shading decisions. — Journal of Travel Medicine 2025 (PubMed 40757771); Arab News Hajj 2025 tech coverage

**الفجوة/الفرصة:** No public evidence of a data layer that measures where and when the courtyard floors and transit routes actually exceed safe barefoot temperatures, nor of shade being allocated dynamically to where crowds move. **اتجاهات الحل:**
- **ظِلّ: خريطة الراحة الحرارية والمسار المُظلَّل** (Zill: thermal-comfort map and shaded-route guidance): A courtyard digital twin that combines solar position, umbrella/arcade geometry and hand-measured surface temperatures into a per-minute shade and floor-temperature map. Sun geometry is deterministic, so a competitor can replicate it fast.
- **شبكة استشعار المناخ الدقيق للساحات** (Courtyard micro-climate sensing network): Low-cost sensor nodes (Raspberry Pi/ESP32 with IR surface-temperature and WBGT-style sensors) placed on existing umbrella masts and light poles, streaming to a hardened dashboard that triggers rules: open umbrellas earlier, start mist fans, redeploy staff or cooling carts, and push heat alerts to control rooms. Security-by-design (signed telemetry, fail-closed commands) plays to the leader's strengths. *يفوز لأن:* Gives the Authority the 'adapt instantly to sudden weather changes' capability the priorities ask for, using their existing umbrella and mist infrastructure as actuators. *قد يخسر لأن:* Actuating the umbrellas needs vendor integration the team cannot access; without it the demo is 'sensors and a chart', which a jury may find thin for a shading challenge.
- **طلاء عاكس وأشرعة تظليل خفيفة لمسارات العبور** (Reflective coating and lightweight shade sails for transit paths): The honest materials answer: retrofit high-albedo, non-glare cool coating on non-marble transit surfaces and tensile shade sails between tunnel exits and courtyard edges, colour-matched to the mosque identity, with wind-release fittings. Would require partnering with an architecture or materials engineering student and using a university lab for reflectance tests. *يفوز لأن:* This is what the challenge literally asks for; a credible coating-plus-sail proposal with measured numbers would speak the jury's language. *قد يخسر لأن:* The team has no fabrication lab, no materials expertise and no access to Makkah roofs; four months is not enough to test durability, glare and structural safety, and the jury will ask exactly those questions.

**المخاطر:** The challenge is fundamentally a materials/architecture brief; a software team will be judged against physical proposals from engineering teams. · Surface-temperature claims must be measured, not assumed; without an IR thermometer campaign the numbers are guesses. · Any recommendation touching floors or umbrellas triggers structural-safety and visual-identity scrutiny the team cannot answer. · Overlaps with the Ministry of Health and SDAIA heat-monitoring programmes; the Authority may see the sensing angle as someone else's domain.

**العرض المتخيَّل:** Split screen: a live IR thermometer reading a marble slab and an asphalt patch in the Prophet's Mosque courtyard at noon (filmed, dated), then the Zill map animating shade coverage across the day with the recommended cool route from the tunnel exit to Gate 21; the ops panel flags a saturated shaded zone and suggests opening the next umbrella row. Ends with a one-page measured-temperature table from real Madinah observations.

**البحث الميداني الممكن:** Very high: measure floor surface temperatures at several courtyard points across the day with a cheap IR thermometer, photograph shade coverage and crowd clustering under the umbrellas at Dhuhr and Asr, walk and time the unshaded routes from the bus drop-offs and tunnels to the gates, and interview elderly visitors about glare. All of this can be done at the Prophet's Mosque within a week.
