# فكّر للحرمين 2026 — Knowledge base for the participation workflow

Source: five official challenge briefs (PDF) from الهيئة العامة للعناية بشؤون المسجد الحرام والمسجد النبوي
(General Authority for the Care of the Two Holy Mosques) × ديوان الابتكار (Misk Foundation's Innovation
Diwan). Sixth edition, launched 23 Aug 2026 in Makkah. Three fields: التقنية · الثقافة والقيم ·
المسؤولية المجتمعية. Press coverage says ideas are submitted by 16 Sep 2026 and the top three
solutions receive financial awards toward implementation. The Diwan format historically is:
idea submission form (idea, objectives, expected impact, team) → filtering → virtual camp (workshops
with experts) → in-person camp → prototype development stage → final pitch to a jury per track
(previous editions: >2000 to >5000 ideas, ~35 teams at in-person camp, ~15 to prototype, ~10 pitch,
3–5 winners; total prizes previously 500K SAR across winners).

Every brief page has the same structure: المجال (field) → المسار (track) → نص التحدي (challenge
question) → وصف التحدي (description) → محاور أولوية التحدي (priority axes) → بعض تحديات الضيف
(guest pain points) → عوامل مؤثرة بالضيف (human / operational / organizational / spatial factors)
→ تجربة الضيف: صوت الضيف (screenshots of real visitor complaints from X), فيديو (links to
@AlharamainSA posts), وسائط (photos).

## FIELD A — الثقافة والقيم · TRACK A1 — تجربة زيارة الروضة الشريفة (Masjid an-Nabawi, Madinah)

### Challenge A1-1 — الوصول والانتظار والدخول للروضة الشريفة
Question: كيف يمكن تحسين تجربة الوصول والانتظار والدخول للروضة الشريفة بما يعزز الطمأنينة والانسيابية؟
Description: الروضة الشريفة is the densest attraction inside the Prophet's Mosque; area ≤ 330 m², fixed
capacity. The guest journey has 3 stages: arrival via the courtyards → waiting at sorting/holding
points (نقاط الفرز) → entry for visiting and prayer. Current operating gap: heavy reliance on physical
barriers and repeated permit checks, which raises stress (fear of missing the slot). Core challenge:
invent an effective model to manage the successive waiting periods and convert them from "anxious
anticipation" to "calm spiritual preparation" — replace silent waiting with paths of cognitive and
emotional enrichment, managing crowd flow smoothly while respecting wide linguistic/cultural diversity.
Stats: 50,000 permits/day · average wait before entry 20 minutes · daily visitors up to 48,000.
Priority axes: service quality per the Pilgrim Experience Program (Vision 2030); crowd safety and
preventing stampedes at sorting points and merging corridors (any verification-speed variance creates
dangerous pressure points instantly); the guest's final impression depends on the calm and dignity of
the few minutes in the Rawdah and greeting the Prophet ﷺ and his two companions.
Guest pain points: rushed behaviour from fear of permit cancellation or not entering; confusion in
outer courtyards and corridors — not knowing the gathering point or correct route; foreign visitors not
understanding staff instructions about queue organisation or remaining time; women bear greater
physical and psychological effort because of crowding/pushing or waiting at fixed times.
Factors — human: emotion makes visitors willing to push/bypass physical organisation → needs calming,
prior psychological awareness, kindness in guidance; waiting points mix young people who can stand long
and elderly/ill whose stamina collapses if waiting is long without preparation; diverse demographics
(nationality, gender, education, income) and psychology.
Operational: flexibility/capacity of digital verification systems to absorb dense crowds very fast
without long standing and pile-ups at smart gates; batching (التفويج) must balance giving the guest
enough time to pray and supplicate inside vs. flexible clearing for the next incoming batches.
Organizational: precise real-time integration between the Authority, security and field agencies to
avoid overlapping crowd-direction authority; NEED for proactive awareness and guidance messages that
reach the guest's phone IN THEIR MOTHER TONGUE HOURS BEFORE THE PERMIT TIME, mapping the journey
precisely from hotel to prayer spot.
Spatial: heat/humidity; narrow Rawdah and corridors adjacent to the Prophet's chamber → no thick
physical barriers or winding paths possible; system forced to use flexible non-physical barriers.
Guest voice (X screenshots): "where do we find the immediate route in the new update"; "yesterday it
was less than 10 minutes and they pushed us out, we barely greeted the Prophet and they closed the
other side"; "the Nusuk app booked a men's slot for me even though I chose women"; "tried to book but
the app says waiting list, is there any fast path"; "I want to book Rawdah for my parents, tried
registering them under my account, it says register new email".

### Challenge A1-2 — التجربة الإيمانية والتاريخية داخل الروضة (multilingual, preserving sanctity)
Question: كيف يمكن تعزيز التجربة الإيمانية والتاريخية لزوار الروضة الشريفة بطريقة متعددة اللغات وتحافظ
على القدسية والسكينة؟
Description: year-round dense turnout managed by tight batching plans. In the minutes allotted, the
guest prays in the Rawdah then stands greeting the Prophet ﷺ and his two companions — the peak of the
spiritual experience. But rapid batching means the visit ends without the guest grasping the place
and its meanings: no identification/explanation/enrichment of the Islamic history around them despite
the Rawdah being full of landmarks and jurisprudential/historical significance. Real challenge: enrich
the guest's spiritual and historical awareness IN THEIR LANGUAGE AND AT THE SITE without affecting
crowd flow, using means that respect the majesty and calm of the place — NO loud or distracting tech.
Priority axes: balance deepening the cognitive/spiritual experience vs. batching efficiency; the
guest's urgent need to know where they stand and what they see in a once-in-a-lifetime moment.
Pain points: limited awareness of historical meanings of each pillar (أسطوانة) or mihrab passed;
language barrier — no real-time multilingual guidance for non-Arabic/non-English speakers to grasp the
stories of the Sacred Chamber; psychological pressure from staff telling them to move and leave.
Factors — human: demographic diversity. Operational: very limited time per batch → enrichment must be
condensed, fast-acting and precisely synchronised with the guest's stay. Spatial: architectural
constraints of the Rawdah.

## FIELD B — المسؤولية المجتمعية · TRACK B1 — بيئة الحرمين الشريفين (environment)

### Challenge B1-1 — التظليل وانعكاس الحرارة للأرضيات
Question: كيف يمكن تطوير حلول تظليل مبتكرة وتحسين انعكاس الحرارة للأرضيات بالساحات والأسطح مع المحافظة
على الهوية البصرية؟
Description: outer courtyards and roofs face massive crowds under direct sun with record summer temps;
traditional shading does not cover all movement spaces; some floor materials store heat → heat stress
and sunstroke risk. Need an integrated system combining smart/innovative shading + floor solutions that
improve solar reflection, without affecting visual identity or obstructing crowds/movement paths.
Priorities: no effect on crowd flow or visual identity; suited to the unique climate; shading and
floors adapt instantly to sudden weather changes without threatening structural safety or guests.
Pain points: extreme hardship walking barefoot on courtyard floors at noon; bright glare from white
floors → visual strain for elderly; no shade when moving from transport stations/tunnels to main doors;
overcrowding in the shaded roofs/courtyards/arcades as people seek shade.
Factors: big differences in heat tolerance across nationalities/ages; visitors gravitate to shade.
Media: 4 @AlharamainSA posts (umbrellas etc.).

### Challenge B1-2 — حواجز إدارة الحشود
Question: كيف يمكن تصميم حواجز لإدارة الحشود في الحرمين الشريفين وساحاتهما تتناسب مع خصوصية المكان؟
Description: field barriers are the key tool to control millions, define tawaf/sa'i paths, direct
worshippers to expansions and courtyards. Gap: wide reliance on traditional plastic or heavy metal
barriers — effective but occupy large physical footprint, choke movement at turns, visually ugly and
"dry", hard to clean (dust/waste accumulate inside), inconsistent shapes/sizes unsuited to the
spirituality/sanctity of the place. Real challenge: reinvent "dynamic innovative barrier systems" that
merge physical solutions with advanced lightweight high-durability materials → flexible guidance
system that can be shaped and re-engineered in the field IN MINUTES for sudden crowd flows, without
being a fixed visual or physical obstacle.
Priorities: let field agencies open/close paths and redirect crowds extremely fast on live density
changes inside prayer halls and courtyards; preserve sanctity, do not block sight lines, no safety
impact.
Pain points: guest stress when surrounded by many plastic/metal barriers; tripping on the protruding
lower feet (metal/plastic bases) — a blind spot in packed crowds, elderly and children fall.
Factors — human: huge crowd pressure → barriers must absorb shocks gradually; some visitors sneak
under/over barriers → anti-climb design without raising visual height. Operational: light weight,
foldable/vertically nestable so staff deploy or withdraw in seconds without noise; smart interactive
lighting that changes colour automatically (green open / red closed) to guide crowds visually from
afar before reaching sorting points. Spatial: open courtyards expose barriers to strong winds/harsh
weather.

### Challenge B1-3 — طيور الحرم (pigeons)
Question: كيف يمكن تطوير حلول للتعامل الأمثل مع طيور الحرم في المنطقة المركزية والساحات وداخل الحرمين؟
Description: balance the random emotional feeding of "the Grand Mosque pigeons" by some visitors with
the highest environmental sanitation, public health and cleanliness standards. Huge bird numbers in
the central area, courtyards, arcades and inner expansions → droppings and organic waste corrode
marble, gilding and facades and deform the visual appearance. Real challenge: integrated system to deal
with the birds in gentle, non-harmful, non-repellent ways — steering their behaviour away from critical
operational zones and inner arcades and attracting them to safe designated areas at the edges of the
courtyards and central area.
Priorities: protect marble, domes, minarets and Islamic inscriptions from chemical corrosion by acidic
droppings; cut the huge daily waste of water, cleaning materials and human effort; integration with the
whole system inside the Haram, courtyards and central area.
Pain points: visitors' clothes/ihram soiled while walking or sitting to pray; feeding zones become
dirty; worshippers' concentration disturbed by dense low flights to grab food scraps near them.
Factors: visitors' emotion drives random grain-buying and feeding → conditions pigeons to gather;
cultures hold special esteem for the Haram pigeons → gentle smart multilingual awareness that
cleanliness does not contradict honouring the pigeons, but in designated places; random feeding spots
outside the Grand Mosque boundary; open spaces let birds roam freely.

## FIELD B — المسؤولية المجتمعية · TRACK B2 — تجربة الضيف والانسيابية (guest experience & flow)

### Challenge B2-1 — سفر الإفطار (iftar tables, Ramadan)
Question: كيف يمكن تطوير تجربة سفر الإفطار بما يرفع الكفاءة ويقلل الهدر؟
Description: iftar tables in both mosques are a complex daily logistics operation gathering millions at
one moment. Gap: mismatch between the quantity of meals and what the guest actually consumes → huge
food and water waste despite efforts; plus the enormous operational challenge of lifting the tables
and fully cleaning carpets so prayer rows are not affected by leftovers. Core challenge: engineer the
very critical 15–20 minute window between lifting the tables and the iqamah; achieve zero waste and
zero unsightly leftovers; move from open quantitative distribution to smart, rationed, sustainable
iftar governed digitally and in the field, guaranteeing guest comfort and need, preventing waste
completely, and shortening logistics (laying, lifting, cleaning) to the minimum.
Stats: 300,000 iftar meals/day average in the Grand Mosque in Ramadan · 10.8M+ meals distributed in
the Grand Mosque courtyards during Ramadan · 3,000+ workers and 1,000 volunteers daily · 34.4M
packages for manual distribution in Makkah during Ramadan.
Priorities: sanctity, cleanliness, protect carpets, no odours affecting khushu; food safety/quality,
full nutritional value, supervised supply chains and table providers under one regulatory umbrella.
Pain points: hard to find a suitable spot at the table in extreme crowding, embarrassment at the fast
distribution style of some volunteers; taking more than needed at iftar, unable to keep the surplus in
a personal bag during prayer → left on the carpet.
Factors — human: rushed collection minutes before adhan pushes guests to take extra for fear of running
out; demographic diversity; different food habits. Operational: very short peak before maghrib adhan;
dense distribution and cleaning in limited time; demand varies between locations; need accurate
quantity estimation and distribution. Organizational: rules for bringing in and distributing meals;
many charitable and operating bodies; cleanliness and waste policies; security/safety in crowded sites.
Spatial: limited space in some sites; heat/climate in courtyards; movement density before and after
iftar.

### Challenge B2-2 — إدارة الأمتعة والأغراض الشخصية (luggage)
Question: كيف يمكن تحسين تجربة إدارة الأمتعة والأغراض الشخصية دون التأثير على الانسيابية والأمن، ويكون
الحرمان الشريفان خاليين من الأمتعة؟
Description: millions arrive directly from transport stations (Haramain train, buses) carrying suitcases,
backpacks, strollers, belongings hard to bring in or carry. Gap: traditional locker boxes, not covering
all points → visitors try to enter with luggage through doors, or leave it randomly beside courtyard
pillars, at toilet exits, or spread it in a scene that deforms the visual appearance; and above all it
creates security and organisational challenges affecting crowd flow. Challenge: invent a solution that
eases and improves the whole luggage experience from hand-over to pick-up to reach ZERO luggage inside
arcades, the mataf and courtyards without causing pile-ups at outer sorting points, with an integrated
logistics supply chain.
Priorities: crowd security/safety, corridors totally free of obstacles, easier security surveillance
without visual distractions; ease of application without comprehensive modifications.
Pain points: anxiety over official papers, money, vital medicines inside the luggage → resist leaving it
in distant boxes; walking long distances through crowds then being refused at the door because of the
bag → hard return to find storage, or abandon it in courtyards; pile-ups at current storage units after
prayer/rituals → long waits; not all visitors know storage locations/services, especially first-timers;
free storage max 4 hours, not enough for those wanting longer worship; visitors need clearer guidance
in multiple languages on how to use the services.
Factors: demographics; the guest's need to keep some close personal items → smart unified alternatives;
wide ignorance of the no-luggage rule among many cultures; communication difficulty for deaf/mute and
blind; incomplete familiarity with technology/apps; guests arrive exhausted from travel.

### Challenge B2-3 — مراكز العناية بالضيوف (guest care centres → proactive service channel)
Question: كيف يمكن تحويل مراكز العناية بالضيوف إلى قناة نوعية لتقديم الخدمات لتحسين تجربة الضيوف؟
Description: care centres spread across courtyards and main sites, but their role is mostly limited to
answering inquiries, handing paper booklets, receiving lost-and-found reports. This misses a major
strategic opportunity to use these assets as preparation stations and smart governance points that
prevent problems and violations before they occur. Core challenge: move the centres from reactive
problem-handling to PROACTIVE guidance and experience enrichment, re-engineering them digitally and
humanly to deliver an integrated package of quality services, turning them into channels of
reassurance reflecting hospitality; make them attraction/guidance points, spreading required awareness
messages, with a Haram-specific model that fits the place and its visitors' needs.
Priorities: optimal use of care centres to reduce visitor confusion; turn them into field sensing
points collecting opinions, remarks, complaints instantly and feeding decision rooms to adjust batching
plans or services operationally around the clock.
Pain points: guest unaware of services offered or how to reach the centres; technical problems on the
guest's phone (no internet, dead battery, unable to book Nusuk permits) → severe anxiety about missing
rituals; standing at the centre unable to explain the problem precisely due to language.
Factors — human: guests arrive exhausted/stressed → staff need high emotional intelligence; demographics;
cultures differ in asking for help (some hesitate and prefer self-service, others insist verbally) →
diverse channels (self-service, human, digital) inside one centre. Spatial: ambient crowd noise
hampers listening between guest and staff.

## FIELD C — التقنية · TRACK C1 — توظيف الذكاء الاصطناعي في الحرمين (AI in the Two Holy Mosques)

### Challenge C1-1 — الاستجابة للحالات الطارئة والإنسانية
Question: كيف يمكن تحسين الاستجابة للحالات الطارئة والإنسانية داخل الحرمين؟
Description: the challenge is crossing dense crowds when emergencies occur — medical, humanitarian, or
sudden physical incapacity. At peaks/seasons crowds become tightly packed human masses hard to
penetrate, making field response teams' arrival a complex logistical and time challenge. Gap: reliance
on conventional response after a traditional report arrives, consuming very critical minutes. Real
challenge: move response to DIGITAL AND FIELD PROACTIVITY together, inventing technical or field
solutions that are flexible and let supervisors and monitors intervene immediately and easily without
confusing crowds or affecting sanctity.
Priorities: the Sharia objective of preserving human life and dignity; delayed response to a fainting
or individual incapacity in a moving crowd → people behind trip → pressure point/stampede threatening
hundreds; minimise response time in closed and open high-density spaces.
Pain points: injured visitor or companions cannot state their precise location; surrounding visitors
anxious/confused → stop or slow the crowd, some push or take opposite paths; communication difficulty
between visitor and first responders due to language/culture → cannot explain condition or give
personal data fast under pressure.
Factors — human: demographics; very high shares of elderly, chronic illness, women and children →
higher likelihood of health/humanitarian cases in dense periods. Operational: limited exclusive
emergency paths inside the mosques and courtyards at peak; need smart MINIATURISED means for
transport, first aid and guidance instead of traditional equipment that cannot penetrate dense crowds.
Spatial: extreme heat; limited space filled with visitors.
Media: photos of Red Crescent teams treating a collapsed pilgrim; a woman in a wheelchair; a
stretcher carried down an escalator.

### Challenge C1-2 — تخصيص الخدمات لفئات الزوار حسب السياق
Question: كيف يمكن تحسين تخصيص الخدمات لفئات الزوار المختلفة وفق السياق الزماني والمكاني والكثافة؟
Description: a unique human mix differing in mobility, culture, age and health. Gap: services (carts,
Zamzam, guidance, mushaf distribution) are delivered efficiently but with a FIXED mechanism that does
not adapt operationally to real-time density changes in a specific time context (e.g. elderly dominate
mid-morning; youth flow at tarawih and tahajjud). Core challenge: personalise standardised uniform
services and move toward hyper-personalised, contextual services — a flexible smart system that
predicts each segment's needs and re-routes resources and services in the field automatically to fit
guest nature, time and crowd level in each zone across the whole journey.
Priorities: optimal resource investment allocated by need; avoid operational waste from piling
services where not needed; the direct relation between "this service was designed for me at my time"
and satisfaction.
Pain points: elderly/mobility-impaired cannot reach general services at peak because they cannot
compete with faster groups; language barrier when signage/service instructions are in languages they
don't master.
Factors — human: demographics; sharp differences in energy/fitness (exhausted vs active). Operational:
flexibility/speed of moving resources between points; need sensing tech to recognise the dominant
demographic pattern per zone to adapt services instantly. Organizational: shift flexibility and task
distribution — matching field staff skills (languages, elderly-care training) to incoming segments per
shift. Spatial: service type/delivery tied to weather; prayer times shape service delivery.

## FIELD A — الثقافة والقيم · TRACK A2 — التجربة الإثرائية للحرمين (enrichment experience)

### Challenge A2-1 — المعالم التاريخية والثقافية → تجربة عالمية ثرية
Question: كيف يمكن تحويل زيارة المعالم التاريخية والثقافية بالحرمين إلى تجربة عالمية ثرية؟
Description: transform visits to historical/cultural landmarks linked to the Two Holy Mosques from a
traditional introductory tour into a rich global experience connecting the guest to the place's
value and its historical/spiritual meaning through storytelling, clear routes, multilingual content
and interactive technologies that strengthen cognitive and emotional impact.
Priorities: landmarks tie to the pilgrim's faith journey; high Sharia/historical value convertible into
a global impactful experience; diverse guests, languages, cultures, expectations; present history in
modern, more interactive ways; position the Haramain as a global enrichment destination beyond
rituals; showcase the Kingdom's care for these sites.
Pain points: weak introductory narrative of the landmark's full value during the visit; hard to link
landmarks into one integrated story of Haramain history; limited multilingual content; guidance
quality varies by guide, site, time; visit reduced to sightseeing/photos without deep knowledge; hard
to grasp historical events or spatial transformations without maps or visual/interactive media; few
immersive elements that make the guest LIVE the story rather than hear it; must suit individuals,
delegations, families, students, elderly, non-Arabic speakers.
Factors — human: guides' storytelling skills vary; need facilitators for many languages/cultures/ages;
impact depends on personal explanation rather than a designed fixed experience; train providers on
interaction, questions, connecting guest to story. Operational: unclear visit routes/sequence between
landmarks; varying duration/quality; limited booking, group scheduling, capacity tools; need pre-visit
priming content and post-visit continuation; organise movement to prevent crowding. Organizational:
multiple bodies/departments; unified governance of content/narrative/experience; unify key messages
about Haramain history and the Kingdom's care; KPIs for satisfaction, understanding, engagement,
impact; align with institutional identity and Sharia/regulatory controls. Spatial: landmarks differ in
size, location, capacity; some cannot host large groups or extended interactive experiences; some are
inside the Haramain boundary → crowd/security/safety; better signage/wayfinding; elderly, disabled,
families, delegations; heat, crowding, peaks, access. Other: multilingual content; immersive tech
(AR, audio guides, interactive maps, visual shows); balance technical dazzle vs. dignity/sanctity;
memorable/shareable elements without violating the place's privacy; expectations differ (knowledge vs
visual vs spiritual).

### Challenge A2-2 — مكتبة الحرم · معرض عمارة الحرمين · مجمع الملك عبدالعزيز لكسوة الكعبة
Question: كيف يمكن تطوير تجربة مكتبة الحرم ومعرض عمارة الحرمين ومجمع الملك عبدالعزيز لكسوة الكعبة
المشرفة لتكون أكثر تفاعلاً وإثراءً؟
Description: move the guest experience at the Grand Mosque Library, the Two Holy Mosques Architecture
Exhibition and the King Abdulaziz Complex for the Kiswa from display-and-watch to a more interactive
and impactful enrichment experience that highlights historical, cognitive and spiritual value and ties
the guest to an integrated narrative of the Kingdom's care for the Haramain. Design routes, content
and interactive tools for diverse segments; a facilitated knowledge and emotional journey mixing
learning, discovery, live viewing and digital technologies, before, during and after the visit.
Stats: 80,000+ book titles · 9,000+ manuscripts · average 1,500 visitors/month (library).
Priorities: direct tie to the pilgrim experience; high value; diverse cognitive/linguistic/age needs;
interaction over passive viewing; the three sites can become ONE unified enrichment route showing the
Kingdom's care.
Pain points: exhibition — guests pass artefacts/models without grasping the full narrative of
architectural evolution; Kiswa complex — limited grasp of the Kiswa-making journey, its precise stages,
craft skills and spiritual symbolism; overall — weak linking of the three sites as one route, varying
explanation, limited multilingual content and interactive elements.
Factors — human: library staff who can simplify manuscripts for non-specialists; exhibition guides who
link artefacts to time and service context; Kiswa facilitators who explain stages and symbolism
warmly; varying storytelling skills for children, students, delegations, non-Arabic speakers.
Operational: library route must balance quiet with enrichment; exhibition route must aid historical/
architectural sequence; Kiswa route must fit production, operations and safety in an industrial/craft
environment; scheduling, group management, durations, before/during/after linking. Organizational:
unified governance respecting each site's nature (knowledge / historical-architectural / craft-symbolic);
one connecting narrative without erasing each site's distinctiveness; unified key messages; per-site
KPIs. Spatial: library quiet limits noisy group interaction; exhibition density needs clear routes and
explanatory panels; Kiswa complex safety, viewing zones, approach limits; different capacities; better
wayfinding; elderly, disabled, large groups. Content: different content needs different interactive
solutions — manuscript digitisation in the library, maps and time comparisons in the exhibition,
simulation or live display of Kiswa making in the complex — all multilingual and dignified.

## FACTS ABOUT THE RAWDAH PERMIT SYSTEM (for A1 challenges)
- Permit issued instantly and free via the Nusuk app (نسك); citizens/residents authenticate through
  Nafath/Absher, foreign visitors via passport + email. One Rawdah prayer permit per visitor per 365
  days (an "immediate path" relaxes this). Permit is personal, non-transferable. Stay inside the
  Rawdah is field-regulated by capacity, usually 10–15 minutes. Next-week slots open Fridays ~2 pm KSA.
- Guest voice shows real friction: gender mis-booking, waiting lists, family bookings under one
  account, being pushed out after <10 minutes, not finding the "immediate route".
